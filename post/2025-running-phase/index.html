My 2025 Running Phase*

Back in March 2025, when I was in the Philippines for my eldest sister’s wedding, my twin sister forced me to a couple night runs. I was doing them in Nike Air Force 1s, and I would feel burning on my soles every time. I also could barely catch up with her and would have to tap out early.

Fast forward to August, I finished a marathon.

Background

I’ve always hated running as a kid. The most running I’ve done in high school outside of PhysEd was trying to catch my bus by covering an 8 minute walking distance in 3 minutes. I was always winded after, and once I started working out for real, never considered running for cardio or warmups, opting for the exercise bike instead (which you can stop at any time).

Part of the reason why I didn’t like it was that I never thought of not doing full-on sprints. Only when I started running at the UP Oval did I realize that you can just… go slow. That made me last a lot longer and could actually “run” for over 20 minutes at around 8:00/km pace.

Starting out

In my 2025 resolutions, I talked about wanting to lost weight, which I also talked about failing horribly in my addressing 2025 resolutions post. That made me want to start taking cardio seriously when I went back home.

I started trying running alone, but would again be unable to pace myself properly and would burn out early, so I started doing run/walks instead with the Couch to 5K Podcast. Over time, I got better and better, and could actually do the 5K without stopping to walk, and that’s when it became addicting.

Race Prep

I also started joining run clubs which motivated me to sign up for a half marathon. I actually started getting overuse injuries and have to actively stop myself from running. Still, that didn’t stop me from doing dumb stuff like this:

Dealing with injuries while trying to maintain running fitness worried me for the upcoming half marathon, but I thought because I could do the above, I could tough it out for 3 hours.

The Big (bigger than expected) Race

Unfortunately, I found out that I actually signed up for a full marathon, which was double the distance (and double the pain). I knew I wouldn’t be able to last for the entire thing without painkillers, so I took some before the race, but forgot to bring some to take halfway. After 4 hours, it was just pain for 100m before going back to walking. I still managed to finish in under 6 hours, though, which I’m proud of.

Aftermath

That of course destroyed my entire body, and I couldn’t run for nearly a month after that (still did a long run on my birthday, though). I transitioned back to cycling after to maintain my fitness, until snow came and I just stopped with endurance sports altogether 😭.

I’m trying to get back into it, though, but now with proper strength training so I don’t mess up my knees, ankles, and shins again.

*This is post #5 for my first 100 Days To Offload challenge.
